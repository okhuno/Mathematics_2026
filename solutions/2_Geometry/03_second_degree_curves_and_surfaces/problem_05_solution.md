### Exercise 5. Hyperbola and Asymptotes
For

```math
\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
```

find the intercepts with the $`x`$-axis, the asymptotes, and make a sketch. Explain why the asymptotes describe the behavior of the curve far from the center.

> **Why this exercise:** shows the role of asymptotes in understanding the global shape of a hyperbola.

#### Answer
- $`x`$-axis intercepts: $`(\pm4,0)`$ (no $`y`$-axis intercepts)
- Asymptotes: $`y=\pm\frac34x`$

#### How I solved it
- **Intercepts:** put $`y=0`$: $`\frac{x^2}{16}=1`$, so $`x=\pm4`$. Put $`x=0`$: $`-\frac{y^2}{9}=1`$ has no real solution.
- **Asymptotes:** for large $`|x|`$ and $`|y|`$ the number $`1`$ is tiny compared with the other terms, so the equation behaves like $`\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=0`$. This gives $`y^2=\frac{9}{16}x^2`$, so $`y=\pm\frac34x`$.
- **Why they describe the far behavior:** far from the center the "$`=1`$" becomes negligible, so the curve gets closer and closer to those two lines without touching them.
