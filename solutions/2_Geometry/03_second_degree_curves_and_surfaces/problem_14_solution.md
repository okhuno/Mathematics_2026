### Exercise 14. Cone and Cylinder
Compare

```math
x^2+y^2=z^2
```

and

```math
x^2+y^2=4.
```

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $`z=c`$, $`x=0`$, and $`y=0`$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $`z`$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

> **Why this exercise:** teaches how to distinguish a cone from a cylinder by the behavior of their cross-sections.

#### Answer

| Cross-section | Cone $`x^2+y^2=z^2`$ | Cylinder $`x^2+y^2=4`$ |
|---|---|---|
| $`z=c`$ | circle of radius $`\|c\|`$ (a point when $`c=0`$) | circle of radius $`2`$ (for every $`c`$) |
| $`x=0`$ | $`y^2=z^2`$: two lines $`z=\pm y`$ crossing at the origin | $`y^2=4`$: two parallel lines $`y=\pm2`$ |
| $`y=0`$ | $`x^2=z^2`$: two lines $`z=\pm x`$ | $`x^2=4`$: two parallel lines $`x=\pm2`$ |

**Sketch description:** the cone is two ice-cream cones joined at their tips (vertex at the origin, opening up and down). The cylinder is an infinitely tall pipe of radius $`2`$ around the $`z`$-axis.

#### How I solved it
- Cone: put $`z=c`$, so $`x^2+y^2=c^2`$, a circle of radius $`|c|`$. Put $`x=0`$: $`y^2=z^2`$, so $`z=\pm y`$. Same idea for $`y=0`$.
- Cylinder: put $`z=c`$: the equation $`x^2+y^2=4`$ does not contain $`z`$ at all, so every horizontal slice is the same circle of radius $`2`$. Put $`x=0`$: $`y=\pm2`$; each value of $`z`$ is allowed, giving vertical lines.

**Why the radius depends on $`z`$ only for the cone:** in the cone's equation $`z`$ appears on the right side, so it controls the radius. In the cylinder's equation $`z`$ is missing, so there is no restriction on $`z`$ and the shape is the same at every height.
