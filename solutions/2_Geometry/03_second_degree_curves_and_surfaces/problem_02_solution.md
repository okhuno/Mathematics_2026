### Exercise 2. Circle by Completing the Square
Reduce

```math
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0
```

to canonical form. Find the center and radius, then substitute two simple points from the circle into both forms of the equation.

> **Why this exercise:** practices completing the square as a transition from a general equation to the geometry of the object.

#### Answer
- Canonical form: $`(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=16`$
- Center $`(3,-2)`$, radius $`4`$
- Points $`(7,-2)`$ and $`(3,2)`$ lie on the circle.

#### How I solved it
1. **Group the $`x`$ terms and the $`y`$ terms:** $`(x^2-6x)+(y^2+4y)=3`$.
2. **Complete the square:** $`x^2-6x=(x-3)^2-9`$ and $`y^2+4y=(y+2)^2-4`$.
3. **Substitute:** $`(x-3)^2-9+(y+2)^2-4=3`$, so $`(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=16`$.
4. **Read off:** center $`(3,-2)`$, radius $`\sqrt{16}=4`$.

**Check with two points:**
- $`(7,-2)`$ is $`4`$ units right of the center. Canonical: $`(4)^2+0^2=16`$ ✓. Original: $`49+4-42-8-3=0`$ ✓.
- $`(3,2)`$ is $`4`$ units above the center. Canonical: $`0+4^2=16`$ ✓. Original: $`9+4-18+8-3=0`$ ✓.
